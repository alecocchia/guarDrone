\abstract
\justify
In aerial robotics, teleoperation is widely exploited in Shared-Control (\textit{SC}) approaches, where high-level decision-making capabilities provided by the human are required and at the same time robot can assist human operator in performing the task, handling low-level dynamics and safety constraints and improving task performances. Since the execution of semi-autonomous tasks via aerial robotics is often obstructed by the limited situational awareness provided by onboard first-person view cameras, this thesis proposes a human-robot shared control framework utilizing a two-UAVs (Unmanned Aerial Vehicles) architecture, exploiting \textit{exocentric vision}. In this setup, one drone is dedicated to perform a physical contact inspection task, emulating sensor data acquisition or sensor mounting, while the other one acts as an active exocentric camera, autonomously adapting its trajectory to provide the human operator with an optimal external point of view. 
\\To achieve this, the observation task of Observer UAV is formulated as a Nonlinear Programming (NLP) problem and solved via Model Predictive Control (\textit{NMPC}). The NMPC controller minimizes the geometric 3D tracking error to keep observed UAV in the field of view (FOV) at a desired relative distance, with a desired PoV. At each phase of the mission, an autonomous "best" POV (Point-Of-View), chosen according by visual heuristics, is selected by a FSM (Finite State Machine) for the human and, to prevent operator motion sickness, the controller explicitly minimizes acceleration, jerk and snap, ensuring smooth camera motion while respecting actuation constraints (thrust and torques). Furthermore, the architecture features a shared control paradigm: the human operator can teleoperate each drone via a haptic device. If human commands on Observer UAV conflict with the FOV or inter-drones collision constraints, a force feedback based on repulsive \textit{APF} (Artificial Potential Field) method is generated to guide the operator without mathematically altering human command. Simultaneously, Inspection UAV executes the contact task utilizing an admittance filter planner.
\\Simulations to validate this architecture are conducted in Gazebo, using ROS2 middleware and PX4 firmware on the two drones. Results demonstrate the real-time computational feasibility of the NMPC solver at 100 Hz and highlight a bounded geometric tracking error, ensuring the target is never lost from the FOV even under conflicting human inputs. The framework guarantees contact stability for Inspection UAV and proves the effectiveness of haptic feedback in safely integrating human high-level decision-making with autonomous low-level constraints. 
\\Preliminary experiments have been conducted, validating simulation results about NMPC over the Observer UAV, and showing the effectiveness of this architecture in real scenarios, with a very light sim-to-real gap. Inspection UAV for now is substituted by a purely teleoperated commercial drone.
