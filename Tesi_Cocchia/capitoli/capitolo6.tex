\label{ch:results}
To validate the proposed cooperative multi-drone architecture, a comprehensive testing campaign was conducted. The evaluation follows this approach: first planners and controllers are validated in a simulated environment, then the human-in-the-loop haptic interactions are analyzed, and finally the complete cooperative mission is shown. A preliminary assessment of the physical real-world setup concludes the chapter.

\section{Software-in-the-Loop Setup}
The simulated validation was performed using the Gazebo physics engine, fully integrated with the PX4 Autopilot Software-in-the-Loop (SITL) environment and ROS 2 Humble. The multi-vehicle setup relies on the Micro-XRCE-DDS bridge to independently handle the uORB messaging for two virtual standard \texttt{x500} quadrotor frames. 
\\To replicate the computational constraints of the real onboard companion computer, the NMPC solver was configured with a strict execution constraint of $100$ Hz (sampling time $T_s = 10$ ms) over a prediction horizon of \textcolor{red}{$N=50$} steps. The state and control weighting matrices ($\mathbf{Q}$ and $\mathbf{R}$) were tuned empirically to prioritize visual constraints (e.g., locking the relative height and radial distance) while heavily penalizing aggressive torque variations, ensuring a smooth video feed.

The objective of this isolated test is to demonstrate the stability of the optimal solver, the safety of the PID-to-NMPC switch, and the real-time computational feasibility.
\\During this test, the drone performs an autonomous vertical takeoff using the native PX4 position controller. Upon reaching the predefined hovering altitude, the safe-switch logic brings the drone into offboard thrust and torque mode. The only conceptual difference is that after the switch the observer drone keeps the other drone centered in image plane; this is a design choice. Another one could have been ignoring the yaw, that is, compensating for it and shifting it a way not to turn.

\section{Complete Cooperative Mission (SITL)}
The final stage of the Software-in-the-Loop validation integrates all modules into the full dual-drone cooperative architecture. In this scenario, the Inspection Drone executes a programmed approach toward a structure, while the Observer Drone autonomously maintains the visual, guided by the NMPC and the operator's haptic supervision.
\\Moreover, the mission is planned to autonomously change visual parameters $r,\beta,z_{rel},\psi$ at each phase of the mission, according to phase visual requirements, in order to help the operator in supervising the mission itself and to teleoperate the other drone at the best of his capabilities.
\begin{figure}[h!]

    \caption{3D trajectories of the two drones}

The spatial evolution of the mission is captured in the 3D trajectory plot (Figure \ref{fig:3d_mission}) and in the respective Cartesian coordinates (Figures \ref{fig:mission_cam_pose} and \ref{fig:mission_int_pose}). The mission evolves through sequential operational phases governed by the FSM:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Takeoff:} Both UAVs execute a vertical take-off.
    \item \textbf{Hovering:} Observer UAV switches to MPC controller, maintaining hovering position but looking at the Inspection UAV.
    \item \textbf{Navigation \& Approach:} The Inspection Drone starts from hovering and navigates toward the target structure, stopping at a safety distance of $2\text{ m}$ from the wall. Throughout this motion, the Observer Drone tracks it autonomously, maintaining a nominal centered perspective. 
    \item \textbf{Interaction:} Upon operator confirmation, the Inspection Drone pushes against the surface. Concurrently, the supervisor switches the Observer's PoV to a closer and decentered configuration (framing the target in the bottom-right corner of the FOV), keeping the contact interface and the wall in the center of the video feed. 
    \item \textbf{Detachment:} Once inspection is concluded, the Inspection Drone retreats by $0.5\text{ m}$ from the wall. Simultaneously, the Observer expands its radial distance to provide the operator with a wider situational awareness during vehicle detachment. 
    \item \textbf{Return Home:} The Inspection Drone navigates back to its initial take-off coordinates, while the Observer drone restores a centered visual tracking until landing. 
\end{itemize}

In our shared control formulation, the artificial potential field acts as an anticipatory safety barrier: when human teleoperation commands the vehicle towards an unfeasible configuration, exponential repulsive forces push the control handle back.
\\Specifically, during radial teleoperation around $t \approx 70\text{ s}$, the pilot pushes the joystick forward to inspect the target at close range. As the standoff distance approaches the safety boundary $r_{\min} = 1.0\text{ m}$ (with a transient encroachment peaking at $s_r = 0.11\text{ m}$), the repulsive force $F_x^{\text{rep}}$ rises steeply and reaches the physical saturation threshold of $15.0\text{ N}$. The strong tactile resistance informs the operator of the impending collision hazard, leading them to ease the forward pressure. As a result, the standoff distance smoothly recovers beyond $1.5\text{ m}$, and $s_r$ promptly returns to zero.
\\A similar protective behavior occurs along the vertical direction around $t \approx 45\text{ s}$. When the operator commands an upward climb, bringing the target close to the upper limit of the vertical camera Field of View ($\text{FOV}_v = 42^\circ$, corresponding to a semi-aperture of $21^\circ$), the target slightly exceeds the viewing boundary ($s_{fov,v} = 2.56^\circ$). In response, the APF exerts a peak downward force $F_z^{\text{rep}} = -15.0\text{ N}$ on the joystick, prompting the human operator to lower the setpoint and immediately re-centering the target within the camera frame ($s_{fov,v} \to 0$).
\\Along the horizontal direction, because the autonomous yaw tracking loop maintains the optical axis tightly aligned with the target ($|\psi_{\text{err}}| \le 16.5^\circ$, well within the horizontal semi-aperture of $34.5^\circ$), the horizontal violation remains zero ($s_{fov,h} = 0^\circ$), resulting in $F_y^{\text{rep}} = 0\text{ N}$ throughout the flight.
