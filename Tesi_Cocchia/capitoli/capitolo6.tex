To validate the proposed cooperative multi-drone architecture, a comprehensive testing campaign was conducted. The evaluation follows this approach: first planners and controllers are validated in a simulated environment, then the human-in-the-loop haptic interactions are analyzed, and finally the complete cooperative mission is shown. A preliminary assessment of the physical real-world setup concludes the chapter.

\section{Software-in-the-Loop Setup}
The simulated validation was performed using the Gazebo physics engine, fully integrated with the PX4 Autopilot Software-in-the-Loop (SITL) environment and ROS 2 Humble. The multi-vehicle setup relies on the Micro-XRCE-DDS bridge to independently handle the uORB messaging for two virtual standard \texttt{x500} quadrotor frames. 
\\To replicate the computational constraints of the real onboard companion computer, the NMPC solver was configured with a strict execution constraint of $100$ Hz (sampling time $T_s = 10$ ms) over a prediction horizon of $N=15$ steps. The state and control weighting matrices ($\mathbf{Q}$ and $\mathbf{R}$) were tuned empirically to prioritize visual constraints (e.g., locking the relative height and radial distance) while heavily penalizing aggressive torque variations, ensuring a smooth video feed.

\section{NMPC Hovering and Tracking Validation}
Before introducing the autonomous switching references or human commands, a baseline validation of the Observer Drone's NMPC was performed. The objective of this isolated test is to empirically demonstrate the stability of the optimal solver, the safety of the PID-to-NMPC switch, and the real-time computational feasibility.
\\During this test, the drone performs an autonomous vertical takeoff using the native PX4 position controller. Upon reaching the predefined hovering altitude, the safe-switch logic brings the drone into offboard thrust and torque mode. The only conceptual difference is that after the switch the observer drone keeps the other drone centered in image plane, so it depends on the actual mutual orientation between the 2 UAVs; this is a design choice. Another one could have been computing the actual $\psi_{dev}$, yaw deviation with respect to $\psi_{centr}$ that keeps the target horizontally centered in the camera image, and compensating for it, so to avoid completely any reference variation after the switch.

As it is shown in the plots, the system reliably reacts to human operator undesired motions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Radial Distance Violation ($s_r$):} Around $t \approx 91\text{ s}$, the pilot pushes the joystick forward, commanding an aggressive approach towards the Inspection UAV. As soon as the inter-drone distance drops below the activation threshold $r < 3.0\text{ m}$ ($d < d_{\text{act}} = 1.5\text{ m}$), the exponential repulsive force $F_x^{\text{rep}}$ begins to grow, providing progressive tactile resistance. Because the operator deliberately overrides this cue to evaluate the barrier, the distance breaches the hard threshold $r_{\min} = 1.50\text{ m}$, causing $F_x^{\text{rep}}$ to saturate at $10.0\text{ N}$ while a boundary violation $s_r > 0$ is registered. The maximum violation reaches $s_r = 0.40\text{ m}$ at $t = 96.15\text{ s}$ (corresponding to an inter-drone distance of $r_{\text{cyl}} = 1.10\text{ m}$). Prompted by the maximum tactile feedback, the operator releases forward pressure, allowing the standoff distance to smoothly recover beyond $1.50\text{ m}$ at $t = 100.65\text{ s}$, where $s_r$ promptly returns to zero.
    \item \textbf{Vertical FoV Violation ($s_{\text{fov},v}$):} Around $t \approx 72\text{ s}$, the operator commands an upward climb. As the target enters the outer $30\%$ of the vertical Field of View ($|z_{\text{cyl}}| > 0.7 z_{\max}$), an upward repulsive force $F_z^{\text{rep}}$ begins to push the joystick handle. Forcing the climb further causes a transient angular violation of $s_{\text{fov},v} = 1.41^\circ$ ($0.025\text{ rad}$) at $t = 73.03\text{ s}$, where $F_z^{\text{rep}}$ reaches the $-10.0\text{ N}$ saturation. Geometrically, at the current standoff distance of $r_{\text{cyl}} = 2.40\text{ m}$, the optical semi-aperture corresponds to a maximum visible semi-height of $z_{\max} = 1.24\text{ m}$, whereas the drone reached $|z_{\text{cyl}}| = 1.31\text{ m}$. This corresponds to a physical height overshoot of:
    \begin{equation}
        \Delta z = |z_{\text{cyl}}| - z_{\max} = 1.31\text{ m} - 1.24\text{ m} = 7.57\text{ cm}.
    \end{equation}
    The target thus momentarily exceeded the optical boundary by $\approx 7.6\text{ cm}$. Under the continuous $-10.0\text{ N}$ tactile cue, the pilot promptly descends, restoring visual framing within just $1.8\text{ s}$ ($t = 74.09\text{ s}$).
    \item \textbf{Horizontal FoV ($s_{\text{fov},h}$):} Throughout the entire teleoperation session, the yaw misalignment remains strictly inside the central $70\%$ deadband of the horizontal aperture. Consequently, the horizontal FOV violation remains zero ($s_{\text{fov},h} = 0.00^\circ$) and no lateral repulsive force is generated ($F_y^{\text{rep}} = 0.0\text{ N}$), confirming that the autonomous yaw tracking loop reliably keeps the optical axis aligned with the target.
\end{itemize}

\section{Complete Cooperative Mission (SITL)}
The final stage of the Software-in-the-Loop validation integrates all modules into the full dual-drone cooperative architecture. In this scenario, the Inspection Drone executes a programmed approach toward a structure, while the Observer Drone autonomously maintains the visual over the other drone, guided by the NMPC and the operator's haptic supervision.
\\Moreover, the mission is planned to autonomously change visual parameters $r,\beta,z_{rel},\psi$ at each phase of the mission, according to phase visual requirements, in order to help the operator in supervising the mission itself and to teleoperate the other drone at the best of his capabilities.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Takeoff \& Hovering Switch:} Both UAVs execute a vertical take-off using PX4 position control. Upon reaching hovering altitude, the Observer UAV switches to the NMPC controller, locking onto the Inspection UAV and maintaining the target centered in the image plane.
    \item \textbf{Navigation \& Obstacle Avoidance via Teleoperation(Figure \ref{fig:sim_phase_obstacle}):} During the transit toward the inspection area, an unmodeled spherical obstacle (in red) is placed along the Observer's flight corridor. This demonstrates one of the advantages of the shared teleoperation channel: the human pilot uses the haptic joystick to smoothly surpass the obstacle with intuitive lateral/vertical inputs, while the NMPC dynamically preserves visual tracking and distance constraints, preventing loss of visual contact.
    \item \textbf{Approach (Figure \ref{fig:sim_phase_approach}):} The Inspection Drone navigates toward the target structure, arresting its forward motion at a safe standoff distance of $2\text{ m}$ from the wall. The Observer tracks it autonomously, keeping a centered viewpoint at nominal standoff distance $r \approx 2.5\text{--}2.7\text{ m}$ to maximize depth perception.
    \item \textbf{Interaction (Figure \ref{fig:sim_phase_interaction}):} Upon operator authorization, the Inspection Drone pushes against the surface. The admittance control loop accommodates the contact force ($\simeq 2\text{ N}$ with compliant displacement of $\simeq 4\text{ cm}$). Simultaneously, the FSM commands an elevated viewpoint ($z_{rel} \approx 0.78\text{ m}$) with a yaw offset, shifting the target drone toward the bottom-right corner of the image plane so that the end-effector tip and the wall remain centered and unobstructed in the video stream.
    \item \textbf{Detachment (Figure \ref{fig:sim_phase_detachment}):} Once inspection is complete, the Inspection Drone retreats by $0.5\text{ m}$ from the wall. The Observer adjusts its vantage point ($r \approx 2.18\text{ m}$) to grant the operator clear visual feedback of the vehicle disengagement.
    \item \textbf{Return Home (Figure \ref{fig:sim_phase_return_home}):} The Inspection Drone navigates back to its initial take-off coordinates, while the Observer drone smoothly restores a centered visual perspective ($z_{rel} \to 0$, $r \approx 2.5\text{ m}$) until disarm and landing.
\end{itemize}

This validates that the camera motion remains remarkably fluid and free of sharp shakes, keeping the streamed video comfortable for human decision-making.

As illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:admittance}, the raw contact forces are successfully filtered and processed by the admittance node. The bottom half of the plot highlights the deviation between the nominal, rigid trajectory and the compliant reference generated by the mass-damper-spring model given by admittance filter. Consequently, the Inspection Drone smoothly accommodates the external force, preventing mechanical damage and ensuring a safe and stable contact phase. A displacement of $\simeq4$cm is estimated by the filter, correspondent to $2$N external force.
Moreover, thanks to the dynamic behavior of the NMPC, the Observer Drone compensates for this sudden compliant shift of its target. The visual tracking remains robust throughout the entire interaction, validating the performance of the proposed multi-drone framework.
