In recent years, the deployment of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in high-risk scenarios and Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) has gained significant relevance in robotics research~\cite{siciliano2008springer}. Structural health monitoring tasks, such as the routine inspection of aging bridges and civil infrastructures, require reliable physical interaction for sensor positioning and structure monitoring, where aerial robots can effectively substitute human workers in hazardous and high-elevation workspaces~\cite{hao2024bilateral, dangelo2025semi_platform}. However, achieving fully autonomous execution in unstructured environments remains an open challenge due to environmental uncertainties, dynamic obstacles, and unexpected mission anomalies.
\\To bridge the gap between human cognitive judgment and robotic execution, shared control (SC) and shared autonomy (SA) architectures have become predominant paradigms~\cite{selvaggio2021autonomy}. Shared control allows a remote operator to take high-level decisions while the autonomous system maintains stability and ensures constraint satisfaction. In bilateral teleoperation frameworks~\cite{hokayem2006bilateral}, incorporating haptic feedback provides the remote pilot with an intuitive physical rendering of environmental interaction forces and artificial safety barriers~\cite{hao2024bilateral,dangelo2025semi}. 
Artificial Potential Fields (APF)~\cite{khatib1986real} are often used inside mobile vehicle control architectures to perform obstacle avoidance~\cite{franchi2012shared}. Nevertheless, within this domain, several haptic-enabled architectures utilize APF method and virtual fixtures to prevent collisions or restrict operators from entering hazardous areas, relying on repulsive forces computed from workspace boundaries~\cite{rosenberg1993virtual,Lam2009APFteleop}.
\\Despite the advantages of shared control, performing aerial contact tasks is heavily constrained by perceptual limitations. Traditionally, aerial teleoperation relies on an egocentric perspective, where the operator supervises the scene exclusively through an onboard First-Person View (FPV) camera~\cite{jarvis2025first}. During physical contact maneuvers against vertical surfaces, this perspective inevitably leads to total visual occlusion, complete loss of 3D depth perception, and loss of situational awareness. Furthermore, standard underactuated quadrotors must tilt to move and to exert forces, causing significant video shake and induced motion sickness for the human operator. In multi-robot coordination ~\cite{uhde2024allocentric}, shifting from an egocentric to an allocentric (or exocentric) perspective drastically reduces the operator's cognitive load, eliminates occlusions, and restores proper spatial understanding. In the context of aerial active observation, several works study how an independent UAV can continuously replan its trajectory to keep a moving target inside its Field of View (FOV) while avoiding occlusions~\cite{penin2018vision, falanga2018pampc}.
\\To provide active exocentric tracking while handling physical actuation bounds and tracking constraints, Model Predictive Control (MPC) represents an established standard in the literature. Works~\cite{jacquet2021motor} demonstrated that perception-constrained Nonlinear MPC (NMPC) can explicitly enforce camera FOV limitations while respecting torque and thrust constraints. Similarly, predictive formulations incorporating higher-order kinematic derivatives, such as jerk and snap, have been investigated to ensure reactive yet smooth multi-rotor motion~\cite{mellinger2011minimum, shayan2025exponential}. Moreover, to operate effectively in real-world scenarios without dedicated force sensors, robust flight control architectures frequently integrate disturbance observers, such as Momentum-Based Estimators (MBE), to estimate unmodeled external wrenches and aerodynamic effects in real time~\cite{ruggiero2015passivity, tomic2014external}.
\\Finally, regarding aerial physical interaction (APhI), the literature abounds in fully actuated platforms equipped with tiltable rotors or aerial manipulators implementing direct impedance control~\cite{dangelo2025semi_platform}; on a planner level, other works on underactuated standard quadrotors rely on high-level compliance strategies, such as admittance filters, to absorb impact forces and maintain stable contact against rigid structures~\cite{Alharbat2025pred_admittance, Augugliaro2013adm_human_quad_interaction}.
\\Building upon these established methodologies, the framework developed in this thesis adopts a specific set of architectural choices. Instead of relying on a single aerial manipulator or an egocentric camera, the mission is decoupled between a dedicated Inspection UAV executing physical contact and an Observer UAV acting as an active exocentric camera, completely separating the video stream from contact vibrations and dynamic tilting. For the observer platform, an NMPC scheme is formulated to actively track the target while directly commanding body thrust and torques in offboard mode, departing from approaches that rely solely on cascaded position controllers. To ensure smooth camera movements and prevent operator visual fatigue, the optimization cost explicitly penalizes linear and angular accelerations, jerk, and snap, while an online Momentum-Based Estimator (MBE) feeds estimated disturbances directly into the solver parameters to eliminate steady-state errors. Furthermore, the architecture exploits Shared Control incorporating online Point of View (PoV) adjustments commanded by the operator via a haptic device, where geometric FOV boundaries and safety distances are mapped into continuous repulsive forces via artificial potential fields to guide the operator without mathematically overriding or blending their commands, and teleoperating the other drone for performing the inspection task. For the inspection platform, stable surface contact is achieved through a high-level admittance filter integrated with the standard PX4 autopilot, ensuring compliant interaction on standard underactuated hardware. The whole framework aims to show the utility of an exocentric view to improve operator's situational awareness in human-drone shared control architectures.
